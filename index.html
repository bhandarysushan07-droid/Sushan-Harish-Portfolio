About

My background runs mechanical engineering, then aerospace: a BEng in mechanical engineering, an MSc in aerospace engineering carrying the School of Engineering's Best Postgraduate Project award, and just over a year split between a regulated MRO floor and a systems/test engineering consultancy building and validating aerospace test rigs.

What connects the disciplines is the same instinct: get underneath the number. Whether that's reconciling a torque reading between Magtrol and a PicoLogger channel, tracing a Non-Conformance Report back to root cause on an aircraft wheel line, or validating a CFD solution against a known benchmark, the job is the same — build the rig or analysis that establishes reliable results, then document it so others can reproduce and trust the work.

I'm currently available for contract, FTC or permanent roles in systems engineering, test engineering, quality engineering or manufacturing engineering, in the UK or India.

Award-winning — MSc Research

Mechanical performance of a Rockwool-reinforced epoxy composite

MSc Dissertation · Kingston University · Winner, Best Postgraduate Project — School of Engineering

Context

An experimental investigation into whether mineral-wool waste material — Rockwool RW3 slab, roughly 75 kg/m³ — could function as a reinforcement in an EL2 epoxy / AT30 hardener system, as a route toward lower-cost, more sustainable composite reinforcement for structural applications.

What I did

  • Prepared unreinforced and Rockwool-reinforced epoxy specimens across multiple fibre orientations and configurations.
  • Ran three-point bending (flexural) and tensile testing to characterise mechanical performance relative to the unreinforced baseline.
  • Analysed how reinforcement orientation and configuration affected flexural stiffness and tensile strength, and interpreted the failure behaviour behind the numbers.
  • Built a complementary ANSYS structural model of the same specimens and compared its predicted stiffness against the experimental results — the two tracked closely, which gave confidence in both the test data and the model.
  • Delivered recommendations on structural reliability and applicability to aerospace-relevant composite design.

Measured results

The unreinforced resin baseline measured a flexural modulus of approximately 0.85 GPa. Depending on reinforcement orientation, reinforced specimens reached flexural modulus values of approximately 6.1–7.6 GPa in the best-performing configurations — with corresponding tensile-strength gains also observed. These figures describe specific tested configurations rather than a general property of Rockwool reinforcement, and orientation had a marked effect on the result.

What it demonstrates

Independent experimental design, materials characterisation, and — the reason it won the School's Best Postgraduate Project prize — the ability to turn a raw dataset into a defensible engineering conclusion.

Computational Aerodynamics

ONERA M6 wing — transonic CFD validation

MSc coursework · ANSYS Fluent

Context

The ONERA M6 is a standard benchmark geometry for validating transonic CFD solvers against known aerodynamic behaviour, including its characteristic shock structure. I ran a Reynolds-Averaged Navier–Stokes simulation of the wing to practise setting up, running, and critically interpreting a transonic CFD case rather than treating the solver as a black box.

Setup

  • Solver: ANSYS Fluent, Spalart–Allmaras turbulence model.
  • Flow conditions: Mach ≈ 0.84, angle of attack ≈ 3.06°, Reynolds number ≈ 11.72 million.
  • Reviewed resulting surface pressure distribution and shock location against the transonic behaviour expected for this geometry.

Result

The simulation returned a lift coefficient (CL) of approximately 0.259 and a drag coefficient (CD) of approximately 0.019 at these conditions.

What it demonstrates

Working knowledge of turbulence modelling, mesh and solver settings for transonic flow, and — just as importantly — checking a CFD result against known aerodynamic behaviour rather than accepting the first number the solver returns.

For background on the benchmark itself, NASA's Turbulence Modeling Resource maintains a public reference case for the ONERA M6 wing.
